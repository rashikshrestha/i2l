Enriching 3D Scene Representation via Interactive Robotic Exploration

Anonymous Author(s)

Abstract

Effective robotic manipulation requires a robust and accurate understanding of the operating environment. While current systems leverage multi-modal sensing to estimate environmental states, passive observation often yields incomplete representations due to occlusions, novel object dynamics, or sensor limitations. To address this, we draw inspiration from human cognitive development, where active physical interaction is used to resolve ambiguity. In this work, we propose a novel framework that leverages online, exploratory experience to dynamically refine a robot's state representation. By actively probing the environment, the robot uncovers latent dynamics and physical properties that are unobservable through passive sensing alone. We demonstrate how this interactively grounded representation is subsequently used to adapt the robot's policy on the fly. We evaluate our method on single-arm pushing tasks involving articulated objects, demonstrating a good improvement in success rate over passive-only baselines.
